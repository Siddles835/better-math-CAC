# Explore design notes (Prompt C)

These notes are written in our own words for MathLift’s Explore layer. Named educators and classroom practices are cited as design influences only — not as endorsements of this product.

## Why Explore exists

Planet lessons and personal practice already help teachers see how a child acts on a problem. Explore adds a second lane: open number-sense play where many answers and many strategies are welcome. Existing lessons stay. Explore is optional, untimed, and reachable from Home and the planet hub (including individual mode).

## Number talks

A number talk is a short, talk-rich moment around one image or expression. The adult (or here, the app) asks what children notice and how they see quantity, then invites other strategies. MathLift’s Number Talks activity mirrors that shape with a calm prompt, tap-able observation chips, and an optional speak-or-draw response — without racing to a single keyed answer.

## Subitizing (“quick look”)

Subitizing is recognizing a small set at a glance, or seeing a larger set as structured groups. Classroom “quick looks” flash or hold a dot pattern and ask how the child saw it. Our Quick Look activity can flash or stay visible, offers “show it again,” and never scores speed. The name describes the glance, not a countdown.

## Ten-frames and rekenreks

Ten-frames (two rows of five) and rekenrek-style bead rows make tens and fives visible. Children compose and decompose amounts with structure instead of only counting one-by-one. Show Me the Number and Make 10/20/100 lean on these concrete layouts before symbols.

## Number lines

A number line supports place, compare, hop, and jump-by-tens thinking. Number Line Explorer lets children move a marker, compare positions, and take ten-jumps without a timed race along the line.

## “How many do you see and how do you see it?”

This classic noticing question separates quantity from strategy. Explore prompts ask for both amount and structure (groups, tens, symmetry). Celebration targets thinking and additional ways, not only a final numeral.

## Decomposing numbers

Part–whole work (for example, 8 as 5+3 or 4+4) builds flexible addition and subtraction. Make 10 (or 20/100) and Show Me the Number collect distinct compositions the child finds and list them — many right answers.

## “Which one doesn’t belong?”

WODB tasks present several images where more than one choice can be justified. The point is the reason, not a single key. Our WODB activity accepts several valid selections with reason chips.

## Open middle and low-floor high-ceiling tasks

Open-middle problems have a clear start and goal with many productive paths. Low-floor high-ceiling tasks let every learner enter and still stretch. Explore generators seed varied prompts so visits differ, while keeping entry concrete (counters first) and ceiling open (“another way?”).

## Marilyn Burns (Math Solutions)

Burns’s classroom work emphasizes problem-rich talk, listening to children’s strategies, and valuing multiple methods. Explore borrows that stance: notice/ask prompts, many ways, and teacher-facing Methods language about investigation — without claiming Math Solutions affiliation.

## Peggy Kaye (games for math)

Kaye’s games treat math as playful and relational at home and school. Explore keeps threat low: optional challenge, gentle collection saves, no loss language, solar-system framing as invitation rather than fuel-running-out pressure.

## Concrete–Representational–Abstract (CRA)

CRA sequences move from objects, to pictures/diagrams, to symbols. Explore defaults to counters, frames, and pictures first; equations and drawn digits appear as optional representations the child can choose, not the only door in.

## Anxiety and dyscalculia-aware constraints

- No manufactured urgency: no countdown timers, speed scores, streak pressure, timed rounds, red failure screens, or loss language.
- Pair numerals with visible quantity.
- Optional read-aloud; choice of representation; keyboard and screen-reader paths; respect reduced motion.
- Math notation stays left-to-right even in RTL UI.
- Coarse exploration signals (activity id, ways-found count, timestamp) may join on-device progress and follow the same deletion rules. No new diagnosis codes.

## What we deliberately do not copy

We are not rebuilding a full virtual-manipulative studio or importing external applets. There is no web access inside Explore and no network calls for these activities. Diagnosis-bearing planet lessons remain the classroom edge; Explore feeds curiosity and number sense beside them.
